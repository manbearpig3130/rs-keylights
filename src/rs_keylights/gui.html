      Pedal
        
      
      
      
        The audio input that receives the
            guitar pedal's output (for example, input 11 on the Blender). It feeds the Pedal meter above and the tone
            check's recordings. The meters show the current level, a short peak hold (the tick) and the loudest level
            of the last 15 seconds; CLIP appears when the signal reaches full scale.
      
      
      
        Tone checkCompares your tone with the record's. Play along with a split song (here, or in Rocksmith
            while following) while the pedal is recorded; only the moments where your notes match the record's guitar
            are compared, so missed or wrong notes, noodling and silence are ignored, and slightly loose timing or a
            slower speed is fine. The result shows how the record's tone differs from yours across the spectrum, and
            whether yours is more or less distorted (a rough guide, as recorded guitars are often double-tracked). Ten
            matched seconds are enough; a riff you know well works best. Every check is kept, so what stays the same
            across songs (the pedal's input level) can be told apart from what differs (each preset).

        The
          Launchkey's keys play the Casio (on channel 1, as a separate part) or another MIDI output (Plays on), and
          light the keys they play, taking priority over a song's lights until released. Their tone is set at the top
          of the Keyboard card with the falling notes, whose keys play the same way. On the Launchkey, knob 7 steps
          through the tones (knob 8 through tone groups while one is shown), and the pads below it turn the keys on
          and off and choose whether they light.

        Tone changes
        When a song changes
            tone (clean, distortion, acoustic…, detected from the tone's name or its effects), the part
            switches to the tone set for that type here, and back to its own tone when the song returns to its first
            tone. Palm-mute and pinch-harmonic tones are unaffected.
        
      
      
      
      
      
        Pinch harmonicsHow pinch harmonics sound: the fretted note, quieter, under a squeal on the part's
          pinch-harmonic tone, struck slightly flat and scooped up to pitch, then a wide vibrato. Short ones (in
          riffs) end before the vibrato starts. Try it plays a riff with a short and a held pinch harmonic on
          the lead parts' tones.

      Adds the song's vocals as a separate
            part (Vocals) to play or light on the keys. The pitch is tracked on the split server, following the lyrics

      When a
            split song's two guitarists are panned to opposite sides (measured from the lead and rhythm charts, which
